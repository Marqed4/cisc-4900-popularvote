Popular Vote: Private query sessions w/ AI forked clustering/condensing

The Problem

In complex and/or fast-paced environments the allocated time to ask questions can feel nonexistent. There isn’t enough time to slow down, become personal, or build shared understanding. And a louder voice isn’t the answer, it’s about timing and precedence. Ideas can become volatile because progression isn’t always linear, yesterday we were working on the foundation, tomorrow we’re roofing, and the next day we might already be installing HVAC.

When queries go unanswered, the small details that mattered begin to blur. Sometimes the question doesn’t even surface until much later, long after the moment has passed. And without a way to capture it, it disappears from relevance entirely. The question that was never asked is the problem that was never prevented.

As humans, we rely on context. We make sense of the world by asking questions, by filling in the gaps. We especially trust information that is straight from the horse’s mouth. That means no third party-bloat, and full accountability. I’m proposing a system that gathers these questions discreetly, privately, and especially without judgment. Whether it’s a classroom trying to understand a lesson or a community trying to understand an issue, the goal is the same: to turn a thousand scattered thoughts into something clear, organized, and answerable.

The Solution

Popular Vote is a web-based application that allows people to privately submit questions into a shared session. The system collects these questions, clusters them using AI, and condenses them into a respectable set of queries that reflect the group’s collective concerns. Instead of dozens of scattered, halfformed or overbaked questions, many of which never get asked, users see a clear, organized summary of what the group actually wants to understand.

This gives instructors, leaders, and communities a direct line to the questions that matter most, without putting pressure on individuals to speak up or compete for time. We’re not prompting AI we’re prompting humanity.

Alternative Solutions

There are tools that try to map public questions, sites like AnswerThePublic, for example. The bargain is that they work at a distance. They scrape search engines, not real people, in real time.

They don’t capture the quiet questions that never get typed, the ones that surface in classrooms, meetings, or communities. And they don’t offer privacy, context, or a way for a group to see its own collective concerns reflected back. What exists today exists in the noise, not the nuance.
